# Feature ideas — 2026-10-15

Ideation for the **Head of Application Software** as the main stakeholder: keeping an overview of
workload and of what the developers are doing, what to track and review regularly, and a grouping
feature to compare how groups of people perform for a project.

How this was produced: three parallel research passes (stakeholder needs, group-comparison design,
GitLab data feasibility checked against the current code), merged into a draft, then a separate
critical review that verified every code and API claim. The review changed the draft materially;
section 10 records what changed and why. Nothing here is implemented yet.

---

## 2. Guiding decisions

### Steering signals, not scores

Every idea is a signal about **work** (items, projects, plans, data quality), not a verdict about a
**person**:

- no composite score, no "top contributor", no ranking, no per-person colours or traffic lights;
- person-level numbers appear only as plain facts in a fixed (alphabetical) order, never sortable
  by a metric, each linking to the exact GitLab list it was counted from;
- period-over-period deltas (▲/▼) only at **team** level, never per person;
- each metric documents its attribution rule in a visible "How this is counted" panel, and shows
  its coverage ("estimate on 12 of 27 issues").

Why: ranking MR or issue counts triggers Goodhart's law (MRs get split, issues closed early, reviews
neglected because they credit someone else); attribution is genuinely ambiguous (pair work, merger
vs author, current vs historic assignee); and the product's own VISION §4 rules out *employee
performance scoring* and *automated productivity judgments*.

### History is a deliberate decision, made before building it

Features 6 and 7 need per-person activity stored for months. That **is** a change to VISION §4 ("no
long-term activity warehousing"), not only to the §18 retention numbers — even when stored as daily
aggregates. In a German company, storing data suitable for monitoring employee performance triggers
works-council co-determination (§87(1) Nr. 6 BetrVG) and needs a GDPR purpose definition.
**The works council and the data-protection officer should be consulted before building 0b, not
before rolling it out.** Features 1–5 do not depend on that outcome.

### Trust model

The app has no login (VISION §6.3): everyone on the internal network sees and can change everything.
Hence: no locally entered absence data, no per-person hygiene/compliance views, and team editing
can be made read-only by configuration (`TEAMPULSE_TEAMS_READONLY`).

## 3. Facts from the current code that shape the ideas

All verified against v0.3.2:

- **Fields fetched and dropped.** `get_user_work` (`src/gitlab_team_pulse/gitlab/client.py`) gets
  the REST list form of issues and MRs; `parse_work_item` (`src/gitlab_team_pulse/gitlab/models.py`)
  keeps a subset and folds `merged_at`/`closed_at` into one `closed_at`. Present in the list
  responses but dropped: `time_stats` (estimate/spent), all `reviewers[]`, `merge_user`, `closed_by`,
  `detailed_merge_status`, `has_conflicts`, `blocking_discussions_resolved`, `user_notes_count`,
  milestone id/due date; on Premium also `weight`, `iteration`, `epic`. Not in list responses:
  `head_pipeline` and the time a review was requested.
- **30-day blind spot.** Work is fetched with `updated_after = now − work_window_days` (default 30),
  so an open MR or issue nobody touched for a month disappears from the cache.
- **Events history.** The contribution calendar backfills 53 weeks of `/users/:id/events` **once** per
  user, then re-fetches only from the last counted day; it keeps only `contribution_days(user, day,
  count)` without project. Per-project history therefore needs a one-off 53-week refetch per user.
- **Timelogs** (`TIMELOGS_QUERY`) select `project`, `issue { iid title webUrl }` and
  `mergeRequest { iid title webUrl }` for 7 calendar days from local midnight — no labels or
  milestone.
- **Crawling and retention follow the selection.** Sync iterates only over selected users, and
  retention purges facts of users who are not selected once they are older than 24 h.

## 4. Feature 0a — Foundation: keep what we fetch, see old open items

Not visible on its own; makes features 1, 2 and 5 almost free.

1. Store the dropped fields listed in §3 (one Alembic migration; `merged_at` and `closed_at` as
   separate columns).
2. Per selected user, three small lists **without** `updated_after`: open MRs as reviewer, open MRs
   as assignee/author, open issues as assignee. Result sets are small; this removes the blind spot.
3. Capability-probe optional GraphQL fields (as is already done for epics):
   `reviewers { mergeRequestInteraction { reviewState } }` for UNREVIEWED / REQUESTED_CHANGES /
   APPROVED.

Effort S. Load: +3 list requests per user per sync.

## 5. Feature 1 — Review queue, stuck work and cross-team waits

**Question:** what is waiting on whom, and for how long? Highest day-to-day value at the lowest cost.

A strip at the top of the dashboard, across the selected people:

- **Waiting for review** — open, non-draft MRs with a selected reviewer, oldest first; shows MR age
  and "no activity for N d" (the request time itself is not available in the API);
- **Stalled** — open items with no update for ≥ N days (configurable, default 7);
- **Cannot merge** — `has_conflicts`, unresolved blocking threads, `detailed_merge_status` such as
  `ci_must_pass` or `ci_still_running`;
- **Waiting on outside the team** — MRs of selected people whose reviewers are all outside the
  selection, and the reverse (selected people reviewing for others). This is the cross-team
  dependency the head of department can actually unblock.

Each row: `!ref`, title, author → reviewer(s), age as text, project, reason ("conflicts", "unresolved
threads", "no activity 12 d"). Age belongs to the **MR**; there is no per-person tally such as
"reviewer X has 5 waiting".

The person cards gain two small chips derived from the same data: open items and pending reviews —
the useful part of a "workload board" without a sortable person table.

Effort S–M (after 0a).

## 6. Feature 2 — Due dates and milestone risk

**Question:** what is overdue or at risk before the next release?

- **Overdue** and **due within 7 days** lists for assigned open items (`due_date` is already stored).
- **Milestone risk** for the milestones the selected people's open items belong to: due date,
  open vs closed items, remaining estimate (Σ max(estimate − spent, 0)), items without estimate.
  Facts only; no prediction.
- Complements the existing milestone-overview tool (milestone → people) from the other direction
  (my people → their deadlines), and covers the recurring EA/MPT release tickets as ordinary items.

Data: `due_date` today, milestone due date and `time_stats` from 0a. Zero extra calls. Effort S.

## 7. Feature 3 — Named teams (grouping, step 1)

**Question:** I steer several groups — let me define them once and switch between them.

- **Team** = named, shared, persisted set of people (a person may be in several teams), with an
  optional description and an optional **project scope** (GitLab projects and/or namespaces, prefix
  match including subprojects). Scope is a read-time filter on the per-user facts by `project_id`;
  it never makes the crawler enumerate projects.
- "**Show on dashboard**" applies a team to the global selection (with confirmation, since the
  selection is shared). With a scope set, the dashboard can filter cards to the scoped projects.
- Answers the VISION §43 question "named selection presets/teams".
- Tables `teams`, `team_members`, `team_scopes`; endpoints `GET/POST /api/teams`,
  `GET/PUT/DELETE /api/teams/{id}`, `POST /api/teams/{id}/apply-selection`; a `teams_version`
  counter for UI refresh; `TEAMPULSE_TEAMS_READONLY` to lock editing.

Effort S (~1–2 days). No extra load: only selected people are crawled, as today.

## 8. Features 4 and 5 — Planned vs. unplanned work; estimate quality

### Feature 4 — Planned vs. unplanned work

**Question:** how much capacity goes to bugs, incidents and hotfixes outside the plan?

Stacked bar of logged time (7 days) per person and for the selection: *in an active milestone* /
*other or no milestone* / *bug or incident*; hover lists the items. A capacity argument for upper
management built on the `Prio::` and type labels the department already maintains.

- Data: extend `TIMELOGS_QUERY` with `labels`, `milestone` (and `type` on `issue` only) — same
  request, more fields; verify against the instance's GraphQL schema first. Label scopes are
  configuration.
- "Active milestone" = the item's milestone is open and today lies between its start and due date;
  a project may have several.
- Labels are the **current** ones, not those at logging time — stated in the "How counted" panel.
- Unlabelled stays "unlabelled", never guessed.
- Trend over 4–12 weeks only after 0b.

Effort S.

### Feature 5 — Estimate quality on work items

**Question:** which tickets overrun or have no estimate, so planning becomes unreliable?

- On each work item a `spent / estimate` chip (`6h / 4h`) and an "over estimate" marker.
- A filter "open, in an active milestone, without estimate".
- A signal about the **ticket's estimate**, never aggregated per person.

Data: `time_stats` from 0a. Zero extra calls. Effort S.

## 9. Features 0b, 6 and 7 — after the history decision

### Feature 0b — Daily history rollups

Aggregates, not raw events, kept for `TEAMPULSE_HISTORY_DAYS` (default **120**: a quarter plus the
comparison period):

- `activity_daily(user_id, day, project_id, metric, value)` — metrics `mr_opened`,
  `mr_merged_as_merger`, `mr_approved`, `mr_comment`, `issue_opened`, `issue_closed_action`. No
  commit counts (an obvious gaming target). Filled from the event stream the contribution sync
  already reads; existing installs need a **one-off forced 53-week refetch** per user.
- `timelog_daily(user_id, day, project_id, target_kind, target_iid, seconds)` — hourly, with a
  one-off backfill and a 14-day re-sweep (people log late and edit entries).
- `outcome_items(...)` — merged MRs (by author) and closed issues (by **current** assignees, the only
  attribution the API offers), incremental via `updated_after`.
- **Team members who are not selected** are tracked at a slower cadence (hourly, history categories
  only) and excluded from the selection purge; capped by `TEAMPULSE_MAX_TRACKED_USERS`.
- Freshness: reports show "history complete from <date>" during a backfill and never present a
  partial period as complete; a failed backfill keeps the last good data and retries, like the
  contribution calendar.

Effort M, plus the decision in §2.

### Feature 6 — Team report (grouping, step 2)

**Question:** how is this group doing for these projects, compared with the previous period?

```
┌ Team report ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [Platform ▾]   Period: [Month ▾] Oct 2026 ◀ ▶   vs Sep 2026              │
│ Scope: group/platform/*  · history complete from 2026-06-17 ✓            │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Hours 412h (▲6%)  MRs merged 38 (▼3)  Issues closed 27 (▲5)              │
│ Approvals 61  Review comments 240  MR cycle time median 1.8d (IQR 0.6–4.1)│
│ Releases 3 (core-api 2.4, web-ui 1.9.1, infra 0.7)                        │
├─ Share of effort by project ─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ core-api   ███████████████ 46%  187h │ 18 MRs · 11 issues                │
│ web-ui     ████████ 24%       99h    │ 12 MRs ·  9 issues                │
│ infra      ████ 12%           49h    │  5 MRs ·  4 issues                │
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [Copy last calendar week as Markdown]   ⓘ How these numbers are counted  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Headline** with deltas to the previous period of the same length, team level only. Cycle time
  as median/IQR, never a per-person mean.
- **Share of effort per project** — the core answer to "how do they perform for a project": is the
  team's time going where we agreed, and what came out of it there.
- **Releases** shipped in the scoped projects (`GET /projects/:id/releases`, scoped projects only,
  hourly): an outcome about work, not people.
- **Weekly digest**: "Copy last calendar week as Markdown" (Mon–Sun, configured time zone) with the
  items closed/merged, hours per project and new Prio::1 items — the Monday report for upper
  management. Copy only; sending stays a non-goal.
- **Person breakdown**: off by default (`TEAMPULSE_TEAM_PERSON_BREAKDOWN=false`); when enabled after
  agreement, alphabetical, facts with links and coverage, no deltas, not sortable.
- **Comparison is over time** (this period vs previous), not team vs team — comparing teams reads as
  ranking the teams and their leads.

| Metric | Attributed to | Source | Main pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hours | person who logged | timelogs `spentAt`, `timeSpent`, `project` | uneven logging; late/edited entries |
| MRs merged | MR author | `outcome_items` (merged MRs, `merged_at`) | pair work invisible; sizes vary |
| Merges performed | merger | events `action_name == accepted` | gatekeeping, not delivery |
| Issues closed | current assignees | `outcome_items` (closed issues) | reassignment rewrites history; team total deduplicates |
| Approvals | approver | events `action_name == approved` | approval rules differ per project |
| Review comments | commenter | events `commented on`, `note.noteable_type == MergeRequest` | includes comments on own MR unless joined with authored MRs |
| MR cycle time | team | `merged_at − created_at` | draft time inflates it |
| Estimate vs spent | team | `time_stats` on closed issues | spent includes non-members; coverage shown |

Invisible to all metrics: work outside GitLab (support, mentoring, meetings). The footer says so.

Endpoints `GET /api/teams/{id}/report?period=month&anchor=2026-10&compare=previous` and
`GET /api/teams/metrics`. Effort L (~6–8 days including sync/retention changes, fake-GitLab support,
browser tests and the 95 % coverage gate).

### Feature 7 — Knowledge concentration (bus factor)

**Question:** which projects depend on a single person in this team?

Per scoped project: the number of distinct tracked people with a merged MR or an approval there in
the last 12 weeks, flagged at "1 person". Drill-down shows who, alphabetically. Labelled "within
tracked people". Pushes are not counted (they reward pushing to one's own branches). Drives pairing,
rotation and review assignment before someone leaves or goes on leave. Data: `activity_daily` from
0b. Effort M. Relaxes "people first" for one secondary panel; worth it, since GitLab does not answer
this per team.

## 10. Review — what changed after the critical pass

The independent review verified the claims against the code and the GitLab API. Accepted changes:

**Factual corrections**

- "A year of events is already fetched" holds only for the first sync; later syncs are incremental
  and only day counts without project are kept → 0b needs a one-off forced 53-week refetch.
- "Team members join the crawl at no extra load" was wrong: sync and retention follow the selection
  only, so tracked non-selected members need sync, scheduling and retention changes and add load →
  moved to 0b with an hourly cadence and a cap.
- REST list responses are not the full objects: no `head_pipeline`, no review-request time →
  "requested 4 d ago" replaced by MR age / inactivity; "failed pipeline" replaced by
  `detailed_merge_status`.
- `TIMELOGS_QUERY` already includes `mergeRequest` and `project`; MRs have no `type` field; every
  timelog belongs to an issue or MR, so a "not linked" bucket would always be empty → removed.
- Issues expose **current** assignees, not assignees at close; event-based "merged" is the merger,
  not the author → metric table corrected.
- Load numbers were inconsistent (2 vs 3 extra lists) → unified to 3.

**Scope changes**

- Foundation split: cheap 0a now; 0b is a deliberate, gated decision, retention 120 instead of 200
  days, no commit metric.
- Teams split into named teams (small, now) and the team report (large, after 0b); the MVP estimate
  was raised from ~2 to ~6–8 days.
- Person breakdown off by default; deltas only at team level; team-vs-team comparison dropped.
- Merged: the standalone workload/WIP board into feature 1 (chips on cards; a sortable person table
  contradicted the "not sortable by metric" rule); the weekly digest into the team report.
- Dropped: per-person timelog hygiene ("weekdays without entries" is effectively the hour-compliance
  alarm VISION §4 excludes, and without login it is visible to everyone) and a locally entered
  "away" marker (absence data editable by anyone on the network is an HR/GDPR liability).
- Added from the review: overdue/milestone risk (feature 2), cross-team waits (in feature 1),
  releases shipped (in the team report). CI/pipeline health is deliberately not proposed:
  project-centric and expensive in requests.

|---|---|---|

## 12. Recommended order

1. **0a + feature 1 + feature 2** — stop dropping fields, close the 30-day blind spot, review queue,
   cross-team waits, overdue/milestone risk (~3–4 days).
2. **Feature 3** — named teams (~1–2 days).
3. **Features 4 and 5** — planned vs unplanned, estimate chips (~2 days).
4. **Works council / data-protection check** on stored history and person breakdowns.
5. **0b**, then **feature 6** with digest and releases (~6–8 days).
6. **Feature 7**.

Open questions for the stakeholder:

- Which label scopes mean "bug/incident" and "unplanned" here (`Type::Bug`, `incident` issue type,
  `Prio::1`)?
- Is the GitLab instance Premium (weight, iteration, epics, blocking links would add value to 2 and 5)?
- Is per-person data in the team report wanted at all, or are team-level facts enough?
